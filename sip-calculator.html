How it works

SIP future value formula

FV = M × (((1+r)ⁿ − 1) ÷ r) × (1+r), where M is the monthly amount, r is the monthly return (annual % ÷ 12 ÷ 100) and n is the number of months. It assumes each payment is invested at the start of the month at a steady return.

Worked example

Investing $500 a month for 20 years at 8% a year puts in $120,000 and grows to about $296,474, so roughly $176,474 of the result is estimated gains. Real returns vary and are not guaranteed.

Frequently asked questions

How is SIP return calculated?

The calculator compounds each monthly payment at the monthly rate and adds them up using the future value formula shown above.

Is 8% a realistic return?

It is not guaranteed. Market returns vary from year to year and can be negative. Try rates such as 6%, 8% and 10% to see a range of outcomes.

Does this include inflation and taxes?

No. Real returns are lower after inflation, fund charges and tax. Treat the result as an illustration.

What is the difference between SIP and a lump sum?

A SIP invests a fixed amount regularly, which spreads your entry over time. A lump sum invests everything at once.
