How it works

What to include on a bill

Your business name and tax ID, the client's name, a unique bill number, the issue and due dates, each item with quantity and rate, any tax or discount, the total due and how to pay you. US bills usually also show your business name and address, payment terms and, where required, your sales tax ID.

How the total is worked out

The subtotal is quantity × rate for every line. The discount percentage comes off the subtotal first, and tax is then applied to the discounted amount. Total due = (subtotal − discount) × (1 + tax %).

Tips for getting paid faster

State the due date and payment method clearly, send the bill the day the work is done, and add late-payment terms in the notes. Use one bill number sequence so every bill can be traced.

Frequently asked questions

Is this bill generator free?

Yes. You can create, copy and print as many bills as you want, with no account or watermark.

How do I save the bill as a PDF?

Click Print / Save as PDF, then choose Save as PDF as the destination in your browser's print window.

Can I add sales tax to my bill?

Yes. Enter your tax rate and it is applied to the amount after discount. Put your EIN or state sales tax ID in the Tax ID field.

Is my bill data saved anywhere?

No. Everything stays in your browser and is cleared when you close or refresh the page, so copy or print the bill before you leave.

What should my bill number look like?

Use a unique, rising sequence such as BILL-0001, BILL-0002. Do not reuse numbers, because accountants and tax authorities expect each bill to be identifiable.
