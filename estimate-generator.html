How it works

What to include on an estimate

Your business name and tax ID, the client's name, a unique estimate number, the issue and due dates, each item with quantity and rate, any tax or discount, the total due and how to pay you. US estimates usually also show your business name and address, payment terms and, where required, your sales tax ID.

How the total is worked out

The subtotal is quantity × rate for every line. The discount percentage comes off the subtotal first, and tax is then applied to the discounted amount. Total due = (subtotal − discount) × (1 + tax %).

Tips for winning the job

State the due date and payment method clearly, send the estimate soon after the site visit, and state in the notes how long the price is valid. Use one estimate number sequence so every estimate can be traced.

Frequently asked questions

Is this estimate generator free?

Yes. You can create, copy and print as many estimates as you want, with no account or watermark.

How do I save the estimate as a PDF?

Click Print / Save as PDF, then choose Save as PDF as the destination in your browser's print window.

Can I add sales tax to my estimate?

Yes. Enter your tax rate and it is applied to the amount after discount. Put your EIN or state sales tax ID in the Tax ID field.

Is my estimate data saved anywhere?

No. Everything stays in your browser and is cleared when you close or refresh the page, so copy or print the estimate before you leave.

What should my estimate number look like?

Use a unique, rising sequence such as EST-0001, EST-0002. Do not reuse numbers, because accountants and tax authorities expect each estimate to be identifiable.
